# **1\. Pre-Analysis Mindset & Framework Foundation (00:00:00 – 00:01:35)**

* **Why Advertisers Fail at Analysis:** Entering a Meta Ads account without a predefined plan causes overwhelm and panic, leading media buyers and founders to make reactive, destructive adjustments (00:00:38 – 00:00:53).  
* **The Goal of the Framework:** Establish a repeatable step-by-step diagnostic process (built out on a Miro board) that maximizes both short-term profitability and long-term compounding brand growth (00:00:59 – 00:01:30).

# **2\. Step 1: Selecting the Right Time Horizon & Understanding the Customer Journey (00:01:35 – 00:04:31)**

## **A. The 3-Day vs. 7-Day Rule**

* **Never Look at Less Than 3 Days:** Evaluating 1- or 2-day windows is the first major mistake advertisers make (00:01:40 – 00:01:51).  
* **When to Use a 3-Day Window:** Only use a 3-day view to check early indicators on newly launched creative concepts—such as Click-Through Rate (CTR), initial ROAS, or initial CPA—to see how they are developing, especially when comparing against historical account benchmarks (00:02:29 – 00:02:51). Never make major structural decisions off 3-day data (00:02:41).  
* **When to Use a 7-Day Window:** Always use at least a 7-day period when making conclusions or deciding whether to scale up or down (00:01:51 – 00:02:02).  
* **Accounting for Weekday Fluctuations:** Consumer buying behavior fluctuates significantly depending on the day of the week. Looking at less than a full 7-day cycle skews data based on random weekday variance, whereas a full 7-day window captures every day of the week for reliable decision-making (00:02:02 – 00:02:24).

## **B. Aligning Analysis Windows with Customer Journey Length**

* **Low-Ticket E-Commerce (\$30 – \$50 AOV):**  
  * When selling lower-priced impulse or low-investment items (\$30 to \$50 Average Order Value), the customer journey is compressed—typically taking only **1 to 2 days** from the first ad impression to purchase (00:02:56 – 00:03:33).  
* **High-Ticket Offers (\$1,500 – \$2,000+ Minimum Offer):**  
  * Example: A client selling high-ticket homeowner services priced at **\$1.5K to \$2K+ minimum** (00:03:15 – 00:03:27).  
  * Even after a prospect views the ad and becomes a lead, it takes **1 to 2 weeks (or longer)** on average for that lead to convert into a paying customer (00:03:33 – 00:03:48).  
  * **Rule:** You must zoom out and evaluate longer multi-week timeframes before judging which ads generate actual converting leads rather than just cheap initial leads (00:03:48 – 00:04:26).

# **3\. Step 2: Diagnosing "What Has Changed?" — Internal vs. External Variables (00:04:32 – 00:06:50)**

Before taking action, identify every variable that shifted during the evaluation window and classify whether it is outside your control or inside your control (00:04:32 – 00:04:59):

## **A. Variables Outside Your Control (External Market Factors)**

1. **Competitor Activity:** A competitor launches an aggressive new promotional offer, or new competitors enter your niche (00:04:59 – 00:05:05).  
2. **Market-Wide CPM Spikes (e.g., Q4 / Black Friday):** Ad auction costs (CPMs) surge rapidly during peak seasonal periods like Q4 (00:05:05 – 00:05:15).  
3. **How to Respond to External CPM Spikes:**  
   * Because you cannot lower market CPMs inside the ad account, you only have two viable options:  
     1. **Launch a High-Converting Offer:** Roll out an offer (such as a Black Friday promotion) to boost website conversion rates and offset expensive traffic costs (00:05:15 – 00:05:33).  
     2. **Scale Down / Pause Spend:** Significantly reduce or pause budgets until auction prices normalize (00:05:33 – 00:05:48).

## **B. Variables Inside Your Control (Internal Account Factors)**

1. **Ad Fatigue & Rising Frequency:** Running the exact same ads for too long without refreshing creative causes frequency to climb. Audiences see the same ad repeatedly, tune it out, and immediately scroll past or skip the video (00:05:53 – 00:06:35).  
2. **How to Respond to Ad Fatigue:** When ad fatigue is the primary variable that changed, the direct solution is launching new creative concepts and refreshing marketing angles (00:06:35 – 00:06:47).

# **4\. Step 3: Action vs. Patience — Why "Doing Nothing" Is Often Optimal (00:06:50 – 00:10:33)**

## **A. The Trap of Reactive Editing**

* When performance dips, founders and advertisers with capital on the line feel intense pressure to intervene (00:06:58 – 00:07:19).  
* **Why Panic-Pausing Destroys Campaigns:** If performance drops for a short period due to an external factor (like temporary CPM increases) and you turn off your highest-spending ads, you break the campaign's optimization momentum (00:07:36 – 00:07:58). When auction costs return to normal, Meta is forced to reallocate budget into unproven secondary ads, compounding your losses and ruining campaign learning (00:07:58 – 00:08:32).

## **B. The Structured Waiting Protocol**

* If performance has been down for **7 days** due to external factors (rising CPMs or competitor promos), **do not touch the campaign immediately** (00:08:32 – 00:08:49).  
* Instead, set a predefined checkpoint: **Wait another 7 days**, and establish in advance what exact action you will take if performance still has not recovered after that second week (00:08:49 – 00:09:06).

## **C. Why You Must Not Turn Off Your Highest-Spending Ads**

* **The Top-Spender Fallacy:** When an account underperforms, the highest-spending ad often displays a poor CPA or ROAS, tempting advertisers to pause it while leaving low-spending ads with high ROAS active (00:09:06 – 00:09:20).  
* **99% Probability Rule:** In 99% of cases, the ad receiving the most spend from Meta is the ad with the highest scalability and conversion potential. If you pause the top spender and force that large budget into a low-spending "high-ROAS" ad, that secondary ad's CPA and ROAS will degrade far worse than the original top spender (00:09:20 – 00:09:45).  
* **When to Actually Pause Ads:** In a CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization) campaign optimizing for lowest-cost conversions, Meta automatically throttles bad ads. The speaker rarely turns off ads—**only** pausing an ad or ad set if he manually forced minimum spend into it during testing and it still failed to convert within target CPA (00:09:50 – 00:10:29).

# **5\. Step 4: Executing the Four Core Courses of Action (00:10:33 – 00:14:22)**

## **Course of Action 1: Scaling Down (Seasonal & Demand Alignment)**

* **Align Spend with Your Annual Marketing Calendar:** Review historical year-over-year data to map natural high-demand and low-demand seasons (00:10:33 – 00:11:19).  
* **Don't Force Evergreen Spend in Off-Peak Months:** If product demand is naturally low due to seasonality and you are only running standard evergreen ads without a special event or promo launch, scale down your budget and save that capital to deploy aggressively when seasonal demand naturally rises (00:11:19 – 00:11:50).

## **Course of Action 2: Scaling Up (Capitalizing on Momentum)**

* **Standard Scaling Rule (20% – 25% Increments):** When hitting target CPA or target ROAS for consecutive days, increase campaign budgets by **20% to 25%**. If performance holds steady for another full week, scale up by **20% to 25%** again (00:11:50 – 00:12:26).  
* **Peak Holiday / Gifting Scaling (3x Budgets):** During historically explosive demand windows (such as holiday gifting periods), a 20% increase is too conservative—you may need to **triple (3x) your budget** to maximize net profit and build cash reserves for slower months (00:12:00 – 00:12:45).

## **Course of Action 3: Launching New Ads (Combating Creative Fatigue)**

* If you have scaled ad spend significantly over time without increasing the volume of new creative launches, frequency climbs and performance stalls because the audience sees the same ads repeatedly. Solve this by launching fresh creative concepts (00:12:45 – 00:13:09).

## **Course of Action 4: Launching or Reframing Offers (Fixing Conversion Rate Dips)**

* **Symptom:** Traffic volume and advertising costs (CPMs/CPCs) remain steady, but on-site **Conversion Rate (CVR)** drops (00:13:09 – 00:13:19).  
* **Solutions:**  
  1. **Seasonal Offer Reframing:** Take an existing discount and reframe it around the current season to create implied urgency (e.g., renaming a standard discount the **"Summer Deal"**) (00:13:19 – 00:13:34).  
  2. **Free Bonus Add-On Product:** If a hero product loses conversion momentum, bundle a free bonus add-on product for a limited time to lift conversion rates without slashing core pricing (00:13:34 – 00:13:51).

# **6\. Step 5: Holistic E-Commerce Diagnostics — New vs. Returning Customer Revenue (00:14:23 – 00:17:12)**

* **The 50% Returning Revenue Benchmark:** To build a sustainable, compounding e-commerce brand that doesn't rely solely on paid ads, you should aim for **at least 50% of monthly revenue** to come from returning customers over time (00:14:23 – 00:14:51).  
* **Separating New vs. Returning Revenue Dips:**  
  * When total store revenue drops for 1 to 2 weeks, check whether the decline came from **New Customer Revenue** or **Returning Customer Revenue** (00:14:51 – 00:15:02).  
  * **Crucial Rule:** Frequently, clients panic and want to cut or edit Meta ads when overall sales dip—even though new customer acquisition from Meta ads is completely stable and profitable, and the entire drop came from returning customers (00:15:02 – 00:15:40). **Never touch a high-momentum paid ads funnel when the issue is returning customer revenue** (00:16:49 – 00:17:11).  
* **How to Fix a Returning Customer Revenue Dip:**  
  * Check if returning customer traffic is down (00:15:40 – 00:15:50).  
  * Activate zero-ad-cost retention channels:  
    1. **Email & SMS Campaigns:** Send more campaigns or launch list-exclusive offers/incentives to bring past buyers back without hurting public brand perception or margins (00:15:50 – 00:16:38).  
    2. **Organic Social Media Activity:** Post consistently on Instagram Stories, run an organic giveaway, and drive engagement to remind past buyers to reorder (00:16:05 – 00:16:49).

* **Weekly Concept Batching:**  
  * Every week, new ad sets are launched directly into this main CBO campaign (00:18:52 – 00:19:02).

## **C. Why Andromeda Outperforms Human Ad Pausing**

* At high spend levels, Meta's **Andromeda algorithm** analyzes thousands of micro-signals simultaneously—including scroll-stop duration, watch time, click behavior, and cross-placement engagement—far beyond the limited columns a human media buyer can read in Ads Manager (00:19:50 – 00:20:41).  
* Even if an ad set receives low spend volume, Andromeda often identifies a specific niche pocket of buyers who resonate with that creative and converts them profitably within target CPA, lifting total campaign efficiency (00:23:09 – 00:23:52).

## **D. The Forced Minimum Spend Testing Protocol (\$50/Day \= 1x AOV)**

* **The Problem:** When launching a new ad set inside an established CBO campaign, Meta often starves the new ad set of budget, delaying conclusive test data for weeks (00:21:43 – 00:22:15).  
* **The Solution (Ad Set Spending Limits):**  
  * Under *Budget & Schedule* at the ad set level, set an **Ad Set Minimum Spend Limit of \$50/day**—which equals roughly **1x the client's Average Order Value (\$50 AOV)**—for **7 days** (00:21:48 – 00:22:33).  
  * Meta can allocate *more* than \$50/day if the concept takes off immediately, but it can never spend *less* than \$50/day (00:22:38 – 00:22:48).  
* **Color-Coded CPA Benchmarks & Kill Rules for This Account:**  
  * **Target CPA Benchmark:** **\~\$22** (00:20:57)  
    * **Green Zone:** Well below \$22 CPA (highly profitable) (00:21:03 – 00:21:09).  
    * **Yellow Zone:** Right around the \~\$22 target CPA range (acceptable/profitable) (00:21:03).  
    * **Red Zone:** Above **\$25 CPA** (outside target, though high-volume ad sets slightly above \$25 are kept live if contributing scale) (00:21:09 – 00:21:30).  
  * **The Exact Kill Rule:** An ad set is **only** turned off if, after **7 days**, it is spending **strictly the forced \$50/day minimum** (meaning Meta refuses to give it organic CBO budget) **AND** its CPA is sitting at **\$27 to \$30** (well above the \$22–\$25 target) (00:20:46 – 00:21:38, 00:22:48 – 00:23:09).

# **8\. Step 6: Mastering Creative Harmony, Frequency & Funnel Attribution (00:23:52 – 00:28:18)**

## **A. How Top-of-Funnel Videos and Bottom-of-Funnel Statics Work in Harmony**

* You cannot judge ads in isolation; you must evaluate how different formats work together in **harmony** across the buyer journey (00:24:07 – 00:24:24).  
* **Analyzing the Account's High-Frequency / High-ROAS Ad Sets:**  
  * Inside the account, certain ad sets show a higher frequency (e.g., **2.5+ frequency**) alongside an exceptional **3.0+ ROAS** and ultra-low CPA (00:24:24 – 00:24:48).  
  * These ad sets contain **static image ads** rather than video ads (00:25:00 – 00:25:05).

## **B. Why Static Ads Capture Last-Click Credit (And Why They Aren't Scalable Alone)**

1. **Top-of-Funnel Heavy Lifting (Video Ads):** Video ad sets educate cold prospects, build initial interest, and warm up the audience—doing the hardest top-of-funnel work (00:25:23 – 00:25:34, 00:26:10).  
2. **Placement Ubiquity of Static Ads (Middle/Bottom of Funnel):** Even with zero retargeting rules applied, Meta's algorithm naturally serves static ads to users who already watched the top-of-funnel videos (00:25:11 – 00:25:34). While video ads can only run in select video placements on Facebook and Instagram, static image ads qualify for **many more placements (including Messenger, right column, and multi-feed slots)** (00:25:34 – 00:25:55).  
3. **Last-Touch Attribution Bias:** Because static ads appear across more placements at higher frequency (2.5+) to warmed-up prospects, they have a much higher probability of being the **final ad clicked or viewed** right before purchase (00:25:55 – 00:26:06). Consequently, Ads Manager credits the conversion to the static ad rather than the video ad that created the demand (00:26:06 – 00:26:37).  
4. **Why Meta Still Pushes Most Budget into Videos:** Despite static ad sets showing higher ROAS and lower CPA on paper, Meta still allocates the bulk of the CBO budget to the video ad sets because the algorithm recognizes that videos generate the initial demand pool that statics merely harvest (00:26:37 – 00:27:01).

## **C. Golden Structural Rule for Andromeda in 2026**

* **Never Mix Top-of-Funnel and Bottom-of-Funnel Ads in the Same Ad Set:** Because Meta's optimization learning happens at the **ad set level**, always separate cold persona/top-of-funnel video concepts and context-heavy/bottom-of-funnel static ads into **distinct ad sets** within your CBO campaign (00:27:01 – 00:27:28).  
* **Hands-Off Budget Allocation:** Once structured in separate ad sets, let Andromeda dynamically distribute spend across the funnel stages without manual budget interference to achieve maximum full-campaign profitability (00:27:28 – 00:27:51).
